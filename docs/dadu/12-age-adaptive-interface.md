# 12 – Age-adaptive interface designs

Follows `00-canon.md` (bands A0–A5, Lap / Little / Explorer modes). Related:
`09-main-dashboard.md`, `11-eight-sections.md` (lesson model), `16-assessment.md`,
`02-character-bible.md` (how characters change register), `19-parent-dashboard.md`
(parent controls).

## 1. Principle

One world, one Dadaji, six ways of meeting it. The age band changes how dense the screen is, how
much is spoken versus written, how long a lesson runs and how an answer is given. It never changes
who teaches, where things are, or the warmth of the feedback. A child who moves from Little mode to
Explorer mode at seven finds the same house, the same friends and the same garden they planted at
four, with more doors open.

Age is a starting point chosen by the parent. The adaptation engine (section 7) adjusts the
effective level by the parent's difficulty setting and the child's demonstrated mastery, so two
six-year-olds can be on different lessons in the same band.

## 4. Character behaviour by band

### 4.1 Dadaji's register – the photosynthesis example

| Band | Dadaji says |
|---|---|
| A1 | "Beta, look. The leaf is green. The sun is warm. The leaf is happy." |
| A2 | "Beta, look at this beautiful leaf. It needs sunlight and water. The sun helps the plant make its food." |
| A3 | "Plants make their own food, beta. The leaf takes sunlight, water from the roots and air, and turns them into food. That is why plants love the sun." |
| A4 | "Let's understand how a leaf makes food. It uses sunlight, water and carbon dioxide from the air, and makes sugar and oxygen. This is called photosynthesis. Say it with me: photo, light; synthesis, making." |
| A5 | "Photosynthesis happens in the chloroplasts, where chlorophyll captures light energy. Water and carbon dioxide become glucose and oxygen. Tomorrow we will test whether a leaf in the dark still makes starch." |

The same scene, the same leaf in the garden, the same Dadaji voice. Only the script variant changes.

### 4.2 Gopu's questions

| Band | Gopu's role |
|---|---|
| A0–A1 | Gopu is the playful older friend: points, laughs, names things, never asks the child anything hard. |
| A2 | Gopu asks the "silly" question the child might be thinking ("Can I plant a toffee?") and makes the mistake first, so the child's mistake feels normal. |
| A3 | Gopu asks "why" and "what if"; sometimes the child is asked to help Gopu. |
| A4 | Gopu is a peer; disagrees with Molly, proposes a guess, and the child decides who is right. |
| A5 | Gopu is a little younger than the learner; the learner is invited to explain things to Gopu ("Can you tell Gopu why?"), which is the assessment. |

### 4.3 The animals

| Character | A0–A2 | A3–A5 |
|---|---|---|
| Kim | Fetches, runs, demonstrates movements | Explains sports rules, "stranger alert" for digital safety, environment tasks |
| Molly | Counts, shows shapes | Puzzles, logic, maths notation, dry jokes |
| Gauri | Slow, soothing, animal sounds | Agriculture, nutrition, kindness to animals |
| Dhanu | Comic beats, knocks things over, hops | Comic relief, the "younger one" the learner looks after |
| Mitthu | Sings single words | Repeats clauses, occasionally gets one wrong for Dadaji to correct |

## 6. Learning speed and in-lesson controls

### 6.1 Speed settings (parent sets defaults; A3+ can change narration speed themselves)

| Setting | Very Slow | Slow | Normal | Fast | Very Fast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Narration playback rate | 0.75× | 0.9× | 1.0× | 1.15× | 1.3× |
| Pause after a sentence | 1.2 s | 0.8 s | 0.5 s | 0.3 s | 0.15 s |
| Lesson progression | every step waits for a tap; examples doubled | step waits for a tap | auto-advance after audio | auto-advance, fewer examples | auto-advance, skips the second example |
| Animation speed | 0.7× | 0.85× | 1.0× | 1.2× | 1.4× (reduce-motion clamps at 1.0×) |

The three controls (narration, progression, animation) are independent. Defaults by band:
A0–A1 Slow / Very Slow / Slow; A2 Slow / Slow / Normal; A3–A5 Normal across the board.

### 6.2 In-lesson controls

| Control | What it does | Little mode | Explorer mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repeat | Replays the current step's audio and animation | Big ear icon, always visible | Always visible |
| Simpler | Plays the explanation variant one level down (A4 → A3 script); objects and scene unchanged | Dadaji offers it after an incorrect answer | Button; also offered after two incorrect answers |
| Advanced | Plays the variant one level up | Not offered | Button; Dadaji offers it after three fast correct answers |
| Replay lesson | Restarts from step 1 keeping completion | Via Dadaji's outro | Button in the outro |
| More examples | Plays the next unused `example` step | After the first example | Button |
| Break | Pauses, Dadaji suggests a stretch with Kim or a drink of water, resumes in place | Dadaji proposes it on a timer | Button plus the screen-time reminder |
| Stop for now | Saves the step and returns to the room | Hold 1 s | Tap |

The hint character (Molly for maths and logic, Kim for sports and environment, Gauri for nature,
Dadaji otherwise) gives at most two hints before Dadaji re-explains.

## 7. The adaptation engine

### 7.1 Inputs

| Input | Source | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Age band | parent profile | A0–A5 |
| Difficulty setting | parent profile | `gentle` (−1 level), `steady` (0), `stretch` (+1) |
| Demonstrated mastery per concept | assessment engine (`16-assessment.md`) | 0–100 per concept, with confidence |
| Recent behaviour | lesson engine | time per step, repeat use, hint use, breaks taken, abandoned steps |
| Accessibility settings | profile | reduce motion, captions, dyslexia font, high contrast, simple navigation |
| Speed settings | profile | the three speed controls |

### 7.2 Effective level

```
effectiveLevel(concept) =
  base      = ageBand index (A0=0 … A5=5)
  + parentDifficulty   (−1, 0, +1)
  + masteryShift       (+1 if mastery ≥ 85 with confidence ≥ 0.7 on the prerequisite chain,
                        −1 if mastery ≤ 40 on the concept or its prerequisite,
                        0 otherwise)
  clamped to [base − 1, base + 1] for interface mode and to [0, 5] overall
```

Interface mode (Lap / Little / Explorer) follows the age band, not the effective level: a four-
year-old at stretch gets A3 content inside Little mode (bigger targets, audio-first). A seven-year-
old at gentle gets A2 content inside Explorer mode. Content level and interface mode are separate
axes, which is how the system avoids treating all children of an age alike.

### 7.3 Rules

1. The engine shifts by at most one level per concept, and only after at least three assessed
   lessons on that concept chain.
2. A shift up is proposed by Dadaji in character ("You are flying through this, beta. Shall we try
   something harder?") and the child or parent accepts; a shift down is silent and framed as a
   different lesson, never as "easier".
3. Behaviour signals (slow steps, many repeats) lower the pace (progression speed) before they
   lower the level.
4. Mastery decays slowly without revisits (half-life 60 days), which is what schedules Mitthu's
   Echo and revision tests, not what lowers the level.
5. Parents can pin a level per subject ("keep Hindi at A2 for now") from the Parent Dashboard;
   pins override the engine and show in reports.
6. No single score ever changes the level; the engine uses the mastery model's confidence.
7. Changes are logged per profile and visible to parents as "Dadaji adjusted Maths to a gentler
   pace on 3 March".

### 7.4 Overrides and safety

- Age band changes are parent-only (behind the gate) and re-run the engine from the new base
  without discarding mastery.
- Moving from Little to Explorer mode triggers a one-time Dadaji tour of the new controls.
- The engine never selects content outside the lesson's `ageBands`; if the effective level has no
  variant, the nearest available variant plays.
- Everything above runs on device so it works offline; the server recomputes on sync and the
  higher-confidence result wins.

## 8. Accessibility across bands

| Feature | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Captions | Every spoken line has a caption in the current language; on by default from A3, off by default in Little mode (parents can turn on), always available |
| Text-to-speech | Any on-screen text is read on tap-and-hold (Little) or via the speaker icon (Explorer) |
| Adjustable fonts | 16–28 px body; Little mode minimum 20 px |
| Dyslexia-friendly reading | Swaps body to a dyslexia-friendly face, increases letter spacing, tints the background to `cream` |
| High contrast | Thicker outlines, 7:1 text contrast, no text over illustration |
| Reduced animation | Static scenes with crossfades; characters still change expression |
| Audio instructions | Every control and every object has a spoken name in all languages |
| Simple navigation | Hides the world behind one Explore tile; lists only |
| Switch and screen reader | Logical focus order; every target labelled; no time-limited answers unless the parent enables timed challenges |

## 9. Checklist for every screen before it ships

1. Rendered at A1, A2, A3 and A5 with the band's real fonts and sizes.
2. Every target meets the band's minimum size.
3. Every string has audio in the pack's languages, or the screen is marked partial.
4. Dadaji is on screen or one tap away.
5. The screen has a "what next" with at most three choices.
6. Reduce-motion and high-contrast variants checked.
7. The same screen tested with a gentle and a stretch profile to confirm content and interface
   are independent.
