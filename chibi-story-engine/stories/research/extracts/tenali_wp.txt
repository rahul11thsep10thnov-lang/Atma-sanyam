'''Tenali Ramakrishna''' (born '''Garlapati Ramakrishna'''), also called '''Tenali Ramalinga''' and '''Tenali Rama''', was a [[Telugu language|Telugu]] poet, scholar, and courtier of the [[Vijayanagara Empire]] in the sixteenth century.<ref name="auto2">{{cite book |last1=Narayana Rao |first1=Velcheru |last2=Shulman |first2=David |title=Classical Telugu Poetry |publisher=University of California Press |year=2002 |page=171 |isbn=978-0-520-22598-5 |url=https://www.ucpress.edu/books/classical-telugu-poetry/paper}}</ref> Born in [[Tenali]] in present-day [[Guntur district]], Andhra Pradesh, he is traditionally counted among the ''[[Ashtadiggajas]]'', the eight poets at the court of the Vijayanagara emperor [[Krishnadevaraya]].<ref>{{cite book |last1=Chenchiah |first1=P. |last2=Bhujanga Rao |first2=Raja M. |title=A History of Telugu Literature |publisher=Asian Educational Services |year=1988 |orig-year=1928 |pages=74–75 |isbn=978-81-206-0313-4 |url=https://archive.org/details/historyoftelugul00chenuoft}}</ref> Literary historians acknowledge him as a major classical ''[[Prabandha (Telugu)|prabandha]]'' poet whose literary career continued into the mid-to-late 16th century under later Vijayanagara rulers, although popular South Indian folklore primarily remembers him as a clever court jester and trickster under the moniker ''Vikatakavi''.<ref>{{cite book |last=Shulman |first=David Dean |title=The King and the Clown in South Indian Myth and Poetry |publisher=Princeton University Press |year=1985 |pages=181–183 |isbn=978-0-691-05442-1 |url=https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691633688/the-king-and-the-clown-in-south-indian-myth-and-poetry?srsltid=AYCX9vKVqJP2t8b9QZeo1YpliVONmNwwLewnNd-AMl5uqKLW4xZd5ePW}}</ref><ref name="auto">{{cite book |last=Datta |first=Amaresh |title=Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature: Navaratri–Sarvasena |volume=4 |publisher=Sahitya Akademi |year=1991 |page=3074 |isbn=978-81-260-1221-3 |url=https://www.rekhta.org/ebooks/detail/encyclopaedia-of-india-literature-volume-004-ebooks}}</ref>

== Life and career ==
Born in the early 16th century into a [[Niyogi]] [[Telugu Brahmins|Brahmin]] family, Ramakrishna spent his early years in Tenali before moving to [[Vijayanagara]], the imperial capital.<ref name="auto2"/> In his early years, he wrote poetry under the pen name Tenali Ramalinga and was a devout [[Shaivism|Shaivite]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Dalal |first=Roshen |title=Hinduism: An Alphabetical Guide |publisher=Penguin Books India |year=2010 |page=412 |isbn=978-0-14-341421-6 |url=https://books.google.co.in/books?id=DH0vmD8ghdMC&printsec=frontcover&source=gbs_book_other_versions_r&cad=2&redir_esc=y#v=onepage&q&f=false}}</ref> Later on, he converted to [[Vaishnavism]], took on the name Ramakrishna, and dedicated his major major mature works to [[Vishnu]] and his manifestations.<ref>{{cite book |last=Shulman |first=David Dean |title=The King and the Clown in South Indian Myth and Poetry |publisher=Princeton University Press |year=1985 |page=181 |isbn=978-0-691-05442-1 |url=https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691633688/the-king-and-the-clown-in-south-indian-myth-and-poetry?srsltid=AYCX9vKVqJP2t8b9QZeo1YpliVONmNwwLewnNd-AMl5uqKLW4xZd5ePW}}</ref>

Popular legend conflicts with the historical record over the timing of his court tenure.<ref name="auto1">{{cite book |last1=Chenchiah |first1=P. |last2=Bhujanga Rao |first2=Raja M. |title=A History of Telugu Literature |publisher=Asian Educational Services |year=1988 |orig-year=1928 |page=75 |isbn=978-81-206-0313-4 |url=https://archive.org/details/historyoftelugul00chenuoft}}</ref> Internal textual evidence and dedications in his works suggest that he survived the fall of the Tuluva dynasty's golden age and flourished well into the second half of the 16th century, receiving patronage from later [[Aravidu dynasty]] rulers and nobles like [[Venkatapati Raya]] and Manuri Venkatadri, despite oral tradition placing him solely as a contemporary of Krishnadevaraya (r. 1509–1529).<ref name="auto"/>

== Literary works ==
Written in the classical Telugu prabandha tradition, Ramakrishna's extant works combine heavily Sanskritized diction and intricate metres with close attention to his characters' inner lives.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Narayana Rao |first1=Velcheru |last2=Shulman |first2=David |title=Classical Telugu Poetry|publisher=University of California Press |year=2002 |pages=171–172 |isbn=978-0-520-22598-5 |url=https://www.ucpress.edu/books/classical-telugu-poetry/paper}}</ref>

* '''''Udbhataradhya Charitamu''''': Composed under his early Shaivite name, Ramalinga, the three-canto narrative poem combines a hagiography of the Shaiva preceptor Udbhata with a glorification of [[Varanasi]].<ref>{{cite book |last1=Chenchiah |first1=P. |last2=Bhujanga Rao |first2=Raja M. |title=A History of Telugu Literature |publisher=Asian Educational Services |year=1988 |orig-year=1928 |page=75 |isbn=978-81-206-0313-4 |url=https://archive.org}}</ref>
* '''''Panduranga Mahatmyam''''': This five-canto poem, regarded as his masterwork and counted among the ''Pancha Maha Kavyas'' (Five Great Kavyas) of Telugu literature, celebrates the shrine of [[Vithoba|Lord Panduranga]] at [[Pandharpur]].<ref name="auto"/> The work, which is dedicated to Viruri Vedadri, is noted by critics for fusing subtle satire, vivid character studies, and devotional themes, especially in the episode featuring the dissolute Brahmin Nigama Sarma.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Narayana Rao |first1=Velcheru |last2=Shulman |first2=David |title=Classical Telugu Poetryb|publisher=University of California Press |year=2002 |pages=171–175 |isbn=978-0-520-22598-5 |url=https://www.ucpress.edu/books/classical-telugu-poetry/paper}}</ref>
* '''''Ghatikachala Mahatmyamu''''': A devotional poem dedicated to the Narasimha temple on [[Sholinghur]]'s Ghatikachala hill.<ref name="auto1"/>
* '''Attributed and associated works''': Two additional poems attributed to him, ''Kandarpaketu Vilasamu'' and ''Harilila Vilasamu'', survive only in fragments preserved within literary anthologies and commentaries, with no complete manuscripts extant.<ref name="auto1"/>

== The ''cāṭu'' tradition and folklore ==
Ramakrishna also inspired a substantial body of oral folklore across the Deccan and South India.<ref>{{cite book |last=Shulman |first=David Dean |title=The King and the Clown in South Indian Myth and Poetry |publisher=Princeton University Press |year=1985 |pages=180–185 |isbn=978-0-691-05442-1 |url=https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691633688/the-king-and-the-clown-in-south-indian-myth-and-poetry?srsltid=AYCX9vKVqJP2t8b9QZeo1YpliVONmNwwLewnNd-AMl5uqKLW4xZd5ePW}}</ref> Within the Telugu literary tradition, he is noted for his ''cāṭu''-extemporaneous verses transmitted orally that frequently satirized court scholars, ministers, and the monarch.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Narayana Rao |first1=Velcheru |last2=Shulman |first2=David |title=A Poem at the Right Moment: Remembered Verses from Premodern South India |publisher=University of California Press |year=1998 |pages=112–118 |isbn=978-0-520-20848-3 |url=https://www.ucpress.edu/books/a-poem-at-the-right-moment/paper}}</ref>

Over time, Ramakrishna merged with the pan-Indian archetype of the royal trickster, assuming a cultural role parallel to that of [[Birbal]] in [[Akbar]]'s court and [[Gopal Bhar]] in Bengal.<ref>{{cite book |last=Datta |first=Amaresh |title=Encyclopaedia of Indian Literature: Devraj to Jyoti |volume=2 |publisher=Sahitya Akademi |year=1988 |page=1312 |isbn=978-81-260-1194-0 |url=https://archive.org/details/encyclopediaofin0002amar/page/n6/mode/1up}}</ref> Across these oral tales and stray verses, he uses quick wit and wordplay to expose corruption, check royal arrogance, and outsmart rivals such as the royal priest [[Tatacharya]].<ref>{{cite book |last=Shulman |first=David Dean |title=The King and the Clown in South Indian Myth and Poetry |publisher=Princeton University Press |year=1985 |pages=186–192 |isbn=978-0-691-05442-1 |url=https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691633688/the-king-and-the-clown-in-south-indian-myth-and-poetry?srsltid=AYCX9vKVqJP2t8b9QZeo1YpliVONmNwwLewnNd-AMl5uqKLW4xZd5ePW}}</ref>

== In popular culture ==
Folk tales about Tenali Ramakrishna have been adapted into films and television series. [[B. S. Ranga]]'s 1956 bilingual biographical drama ''[[Tenali Ramakrishna (film)|Tenali Ramakrishna]]'' —released in Tamil as ''Tenali Raman''—stars [[Akkineni Nageswara Rao]] and [[Sivaji Ganesan]] in the title roles, respectively.<ref>{{cite book |last1=Rajadhyaksha |first1=Ashish |last2=Willemen |first2=Paul |title=Encyclopaedia of Indian Cinema |publisher=Routledge |year=1999 |page=348 |isbn=978-1-57958-146-6 |url=https://books.google.co.in/books?id=SLkABAAAQBAJ}}</ref> On television, Hindi-language television series ''[[Tenali Rama (TV series)|Tenali Rama]]'' , was aired from 2017 to 2020 and returned for a second season in 2024.<ref>{{cite news |title=‘Tenali Rama’ returns on Sony SAB |url=https://www.indianbroadcastingworld.com/tenali-rama-returns-on-sony-sab/ |work=India Broadcasting World  |date=16 November 2024}}</ref>
